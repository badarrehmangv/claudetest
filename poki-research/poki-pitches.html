What to build for Poki

How Poki accepts games, what the whole live catalogue says about what wins, what Blumgi and AJ Ordaz show, and five game pitches ranked by how likely they are to get in and do well.

Based on: data from all 1,503 live Poki games (pulled today), 44 Poki developer-docs pages, 33 Poki blog posts, Blumgi's blog, developer case studies, and measured download sizes for 74 games.

Bottom line. Poki accepts games on player data, not on the pitch. You need 3+ minutes average playtime, a strong thumbnail click rate and fast loading. The final review then declines anything that overlaps too much with what's already there. Games that combine ragdoll or physics comedy, a 2-player or online mode, 3D or sports and dress-up or makeover beat the median. Puzzle, merge, idle and match-3 games lose. My top recommendation is Dodge Flop, a ragdoll dodgeball game: it stacks four winning traits in a niche with zero games on Poki today. Build it and one cheap backup (Sneaker Spa) as 2-week prototypes, then let Poki's free playtests pick the winner.

How a game gets onto Poki

Every game is hand-picked, and Poki keeps daily releases limited. About 4–5 games go live globally each week (197 so far in 2026). The route is fixed, and each gate is a metric:

  1. Apply & uploadThe access form asks for links to games you've already released. Builds can be prototypes.
  2. PlaytestsFree and unlimited. 10 video recordings each, with inputs and console output.
  3. Player fit test500 players, about 5 h. Pass: 3+ min average and 25%+ of plays over 3 min.
  4. Web fit testAbout 10k players, about 7 days. Click rate, time on page and share who start playing, each scored against the category average.
  5. Final review1–2 weeks. Checks quality, originality and overlap with existing games. A no is final for that game.
  6. Soft → global releaseAbout 2–3 months. QA, then 2–3 weeks on category pages only, then about 2 weeks of homepage push.

Targets, not just minimums: 5+ min average playtime (10+ for management/sim games) and 65%+ of visitors starting to play. The average Poki game gets ~70% to start and 6+ min. Unity games "typically land around 60 to 65%" because of build weight.

Why games get declined: clones; games that feel "heavily tool-assisted or rapidly assembled"; anything "even slightly shocking"; and "the portfolio is covered", meaning too much overlap in its categories. "Adds to diversity" means "a mechanic, setting, or angle not already well-represented on Poki, not just a variation on an existing hit." Trend games (brainrot etc.) must add real gameplay, not just meme visuals.

Hard requirements: runs on mobile and desktop; works in portrait ("our QA team tests for it"); instant start with no text tutorials; small download ("every extra megabyte… lose a couple percent"); Poki SDK events; no external links. Stickman Hook went from 40 MB and 50% of visitors starting to play to 6 MB and 72%.

Deal: web exclusivity for 5 years by default (Steam and app stores stay open). Revenue from players Poki brings is shared; players you bring yourself pay you 100%. Money comes mostly from rewarded ads. Sourced figures range from "close to six figures" for a solo developer with two games up to "$1M+ over 12 months" for a category leader (Drive Mad).

What the catalogue says wins

Poki shows thumbs-up and thumbs-down counts on every game. I used total votes as a rough measure of player volume, and votes per day since release as a measure of current pull. It's noisy (plays per vote vary 20–350×), so treat only gaps of about 2× or more as real. A typical 2025–26 release gets a median of ~128 votes/day; the top 10% get 874+. The top 10% of recent games hold 57% of recent votes.

  • Poki promotes new games that perform. 30 of the top 60 homepage slots are 2025–26 releases.
  • Big-brand mobile ports raise the floor but don't own the chart. Voodoo, Outfit7, Fingersoft and others take 7 of the top 20 recent games, but only 11 of the top 60.
  • Studios with 2–9 games do best. First games perform about average but rarely become the biggest hits: 12 studios hold 28 of the top 60 recent slots. Plan a series, not one shot.
  • 80% thumbs-up is the quality floor. Poki's star rating is just 1 + 4 × the thumbs-up share, so 4.2 stars means 80% up. 54 of the top 60 recent games are above that.
  • Keep updating after launch. Top-60 games were updated a median of 77 days ago, against 196 days for the rest. Vortella's Dress Up earned its big homepage tile three months after launch, through updates.

Blumgi and AJ Ordaz

Blumgi (Loïc Roger, solo, France)

Co-creator of Stickman Hook. 13 games, 4.1M votes, median 225k votes per game; ranked #23 of 434 developers.

  • One verb: hold to charge, release on a physics body. 8 of his 13 games use it, and those do about 2× better than his others.
  • About 100 short levels (roughly 1 hour), flat bright 2D art with one mascot, "No stories, intros, or complex menus".
  • A local 2-player mode where the verb maps to one key per player (5 of 13 games).
  • Construct 3 builds of 1–4 MB, portrait and landscape. A new game every ~4.6 months.
  • His hits are Slime (1.03M votes) and Rocket (911k). The recent ones are weaker: Splash is his worst by current pull. Even the best miss sometimes.
  • He killed a year-long project (Blumgi Chase) because it had no pre-production and its scope doubled. His rule: prototype in about a week and cut fast.

AJ Ordaz (solo, Construct 3)

9 live Poki games (plus at least 2 removed), 275k votes in total, median 18k per game. Among the 87 developers with 5+ games he ranks 82nd, so his catalogue is Blumgi's in reverse: a useful "what not to do" as much as "what to do". His whole catalogue has about as many votes as one median Blumgi game.

  • Only 2 of his 9 games beat a typical game from the same quarter: Pretty Odd Bunny: Roast it! (1.3×) and Trapped in the Dollhouse (2.1×). Nekopirate, a story-driven platformer, is his self-described "big flop" (8k votes, 0.17×).
  • A Cleaning Story was his "best launch ever" in his own words, but its votes put it at 0.5× a typical game. That probably reflects playtime or revenue, which votes don't show. The cleaning games that break out are 3D (Robo Cleaner Simulator, Power Wash Cleanup).
  • Trapped in the Dollhouse (a cute-creepy, tap-only escape game) is his best: 355 votes/day and his only homepage slot. It sits about at the median of the strong horror/escape shelf, and only 72% of votes are thumbs-up (bottom 6% on Poki). The likely cause is puzzle friction or the scare level. He added 5 rooms 55 days after launch.
  • Veggie Merge, a merge game with no twist, came out 13th of the 15 merge games released in 2026, as the merge data predicts.
  • What we learn: experience and craft don't carry over automatically. His best-rated game (Ranch UFO, 87% up) is one of his least played, so on Poki the concept and genre shelf matter more than polish. His pivot to "what players want" produced a portfolio of bets with one near-hit, not a higher average.

What to take from both: simple input, instant fun, bright art, both orientations, small builds, many small bets. What not to copy: Blumgi's occupied combinations (a hold-to-jump slime, grappling swings, basketball or soccer physics puzzles, cars with a boost, knocking enemies into water, rope cutting, paint-the-arena). Poki can decline those as overlap.

Five pitches, ranked

Ranked by chance of acceptance × expected pull ÷ build cost. I assumed a small team (1–4 people) building for 2–4 months. Every pitch's niche was checked against the 1,503 live games.

1 · Dodge Flop

Ragdoll sports · 2-player · 3D-lite

Ragdoll dodgeball. Hold to wind up a throw, release to fling. Get hit and you flop across the court in slow motion.

Why it wins
It stacks four of the strongest traits: ragdoll (~298/day), 2-player (~263/day), sports (250–325/day) and stickman-style characters. There are zero dodgeball games on Poki, so it clearly "adds to diversity".
Core loop
60–90 second rounds, best of 5. Catch a ball to bring a teammate back. Solo play runs a bot tournament ladder, so it clears the 3-minute gate without a friend. Same-keyboard 2-player uses one key per player. Unlock arenas and silly outfits, with rewarded ads for cosmetics.
Controls
One button (hold and release). Movement and dodging come from timing and auto-positioning. Tap on mobile; works in portrait and landscape.
Build
PlayCanvas or Three.js low-poly 3D, or Construct with 2D ragdolls. Under 5 MB. About 8–12 weeks. Online mode later, with Poki's multiplayer library plus bots.
Risks
Ragdoll 2-player is well supplied (Rooftop Snipers-style games, 37 same-keyboard titles), so the dodgeball rules must feel new, not reskinned. Physics tuning takes time.
Scores
Acceptance: highUpside: highCost: medium

2 · Sneaker Spa

Makeover · satisfying cleaning · mouse or tap

Customers bring you trashed sneakers. Scrub, wash and restore them, then design them with paint, laces and stickers, and watch the customer react.

Why it wins
It combines the strongest theme (dress-up/makeover, ×2.06) with the cleaning trend. Robo Cleaner Simulator is #2 on today's homepage (1,105/day), Beauty Salon gets 1,349/day and Phone CASE DIY 1,717/day. No sneaker game exists on Poki. The DIY/design half carries the pitch: flat 2D cleaning alone underperforms (A Cleaning Story is at 0.5× a typical game), while the cleaning hits are 3D.
Core loop
Clean (no fail state) → customise → rate → earn money for tools and designs. Run a tiny shop you walk around in, based on Vortella's lesson that players "wanted to control a character that runs around in a little world". Later: a design contest where players vote.
Controls
Click or tap and drag only. Portrait-first.
Build
A 3D-lite sneaker on a turntable (PlayCanvas or Three.js) matches the cleaning winners; flat 2D in Construct is the cheaper fallback. Aim for 3–5 MB. About 8–10 weeks. Mostly art.
Risks
Makeover games are growing fast (WeLoPlay alone has several), so the restore-then-design twist has to carry it. Simulation-style loops need 10+ minutes of playtime to count as good. If playtests show that players only enjoy the cleaning part, it will likely perform like A Cleaning Story.
Scores
Acceptance: highUpside: medium–highCost: low

3 · Puff

Blumgi-style one-verb physics · 100 levels

You're a pufferfish. Hold to inflate (float up, bounce off spikes), release to deflate (sink, squeeze through gaps). Reach the shell.

Why it wins
It follows Blumgi's proven recipe with a verb nobody uses: only one Poki game mentions inflating anything. It is cheap, readable in one second, identical on mouse, touch and keyboard, and easy to add a 2-player race to.
Core loop
About 100 short levels, with a new element every few levels (currents, bubbles, eels). Unlock a new sea creature every 10 levels. Local 2-player race.
Controls
One button: hold or release.
Build
Construct 3, flat vector art, about 2 MB, about 6–8 weeks. It suits your first Poki game, because it's the cheapest route through the playtest pipeline.
Risks
One-button games usually do poorly (a typical one scores about 0.6× the norm) and are carried by rare outliers. The feel has to be excellent. Its upside is lower than pitches 1 and 4.
Scores
Acceptance: medium–highUpside: mediumCost: low

4 · Prop Party

3D online · hide and seek · .io

Online prop hunt in a giant kid's bedroom. Hiders turn into toys, lamps and socks; seekers bonk anything suspicious.

Why it wins
It has the highest upside: .io and online games have the strongest pull (~600+/day), 3D is robust, and Hide and Paint (1 of 16 hide-and-seek games) has 2,975/day as a 2026 release. No prop-hunt game exists on Poki.
Core loop
3-minute rounds, swapping roles each round. Bots fill lobbies, which Poki recommends for the web fit test. Unlock new maps and prop sets.
Controls
WASD plus mouse and one action key; on-screen joystick on mobile.
Build
PlayCanvas or Three.js with Poki's multiplayer library. Under 6 MB. About 3–5 months. Needs someone with netcode and bot experience.
Risks
It's close to Hide and Paint's "blend in" fantasy, so Poki could call that category covered. Ask Poki early. Online games have the highest cost and failure risk.
Scores
Acceptance: mediumUpside: very highCost: high

5 · Hamster Heist

Cute-creepy escape · funny fails · mouse or tap

A class hamster escapes the school at night. At each obstacle, pick one of three tools. Most choices fail in funny ways; one works.

Why it wins
Choice-based escape games with funny fails are all-time Poki giants: Escaping the Prison has 3.26M votes and Fleeing the Complex 2.4M. The horror/escape shelf is strong for 2025–26 releases, at about 1.9× a typical game (16 games). Examples: Trapped in the Dollhouse at 2.1×, and Exhibit of Sorrows, a doll-museum escape, at 2.9× with 4.6 stars. No hamster game exists on Poki.
Core loop
Branching choices with a fail gag for each wrong one. 20–40 minutes of chapters, with a mildly spooky janitor. Collect every fail to fill an album, which drives replays.
Controls
Tap only, in both orientations.
Build
2D animation-heavy (Construct or Spine). About 3–4 MB and about 8–12 weeks. Animation is the main cost.
Risks
It must not feel like a Henry Stickmin clone: its own world, humour and art. Keep the scares mild (Poki avoids anything "even slightly shocking"). The horror sample is small (n=8). The Dollhouse's low 72% thumbs-up suggests stuck puzzles hurt, so add hints and make every choice pay off with a gag. Narrative games are weak unless the choices are the fun.
Scores
Acceptance: mediumUpside: medium–highCost: medium

Next steps

  1. Apply for Poki for Developers now. The form asks for links to games you've already released, so gather them.
  2. Week 1–2: build rough prototypes of Dodge Flop and Sneaker Spa, portrait-ready and under 5 MB, with the SDK's gameplayStart/Stop events in.
  3. Week 3: run Poki playtests (free, results in minutes). Keep going only if at least 3 of 10 players pass 3 minutes and at least 1 passes 10 minutes (a go/no-go rule from Kuyi Mobile, a studio that got in after 9 rejections).
  4. Week 4+: iterate one metric at a time until you reach 3+ min average and 25%+ over 3 min in the player fit test. Then run the web fit test with a strong static thumbnail.
  5. After global release, update every few weeks. Start the next game in the same brand, the way Blumgi builds a catalogue of linked games.

Method and limits

How this was made

The embedded data (votes, release date, categories, controls, orientation) was pulled today from all 1,503 game pages on poki.com. Six analysts worked from it and from the downloaded sources. An independent checker recomputed the catalogue numbers and corrected several of them: the soccer, obby and horror signals were downgraded, and the "rating" formula was confirmed. Download sizes are what loaded in headless Chromium within about 60 seconds without clicking, so they're a lower bound for games that load more after starting.

Limits: votes are not plays. 2019-03-11 is a bulk migration date for old games. The AJ Ordaz video had no readable transcript, so its lessons come from the description and his game pages. Reddit and Medium were blocked. Poki publishes no acceptance rate and no age or gender data. The pitches are my judgement built on this evidence, not tested concepts.
